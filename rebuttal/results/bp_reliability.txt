==============================================================================
b_p RELIABILITY: leave-one-post-out stability by size of sharing history
==============================================================================

If b_p is a stable property of a publisher, removing one of its posts should
barely move it. If the history is short, it moves a lot.

r is scale-free and flatters short histories: at 2 links r = 0.85 while the
mean shift is 0.29 on a b_p scale that spans [-1, +1]. Read both columns.

   posts 371; their b_p takes only the values -1, 0, 1
   They are excluded from the table above. For the 354 that posted their

   single category, so the score trivially does not move; that stability is an
   artefact of the degeneracy, not evidence of reliability. With one distinct
   link b_p is not an estimate, it is a label forced by a single observation.

==============================================================================
WHAT THE THRESHOLD ACTUALLY SELECTS (Table 2.4)
==============================================================================

Requiring a longer sharing history does not only reduce noise in b_p; it
also selects larger, more institutional publishers. If it were a clean
measurement-error correction, the sample composition would not move.

Publisher-level quantities are computed over DISTINCT PUBLISHERS, not over
posts. Weighting publisher attributes by post count lets one high-volume page
enter the average dozens of times and reverses the centrist trend, so both
weightings are reported.

The two weightings disagree on direction. Restricting keeps a few very
high-volume centrist outlets whose posts dominate the post-weighted share,
while removing proportionally more centrist publishers than partisan ones.
What both agree on is that the surviving sample is larger and less toxic,
i.e. a different population rather than the same one measured better.
