-- arithmetic relation between negativity and the Consensus Index --
   CI_i == |1 - 2 * f_neg,i| holds exactly (max |deviation| = 1.11e-16)
   for the 94.83% of posts with f_neg <= 0.5 this reduces to CI = 1 - 2*f_neg,
   i.e. CI is a strictly decreasing LINEAR function of the post's own negativity.
   Spearman(CI, f_neg) = -0.9951
   Pearson (CI, f_neg) = -0.9274
   => at the level of a single post, 'negativity' and 'heterogeneity' are the
      same quantity measured twice. The reviewer's concern is correct here.

-- how often the fold in CI = |1 - 2*f_neg| engages --
   f_neg > 0.5 : 237 (5.17%)
   f_neg > 0.7 : 47 (1.03%)
   f_neg > 0.9 : 4 (0.09%)
   majority-negative AND CI > 0.5: 19 (0.41%)
   maximum observed f_neg = 0.959
   Pearson(CI, 1 - 2*f_neg) over the whole sample = +0.927
   below the fold (f_neg <= 0.5) Pearson(CI, f_neg) = -1.000

-- but the model's predictor is the LEAVE-ONE-OUT content-level score RS --
   RS is computed from the OTHER posts sharing the same link; 1-RS is the
   negative share of those other posts.
   Pearson (CI, RS)                 = 0.7457
   Pearson (f_neg_own, f_neg_others)= 0.7815
   Spearman(f_neg_own, f_neg_others)= 0.7185
   The CI-RS association is therefore NOT definitional: it is the empirical
   fact that the same news item draws a similar reaction mix from different
   audiences. Its size is bounded by that content-level reproducibility.

-- how often is RS really 'a different audience'? --
   (measured on the pre-deletion frame RS is computed over, not on the 4,584)
   (content, publisher) pairs contributing more than one post to an RS : 403
   posts whose RS includes another post by the SAME publisher: 720 (15.71%)
   posts whose RS comes ENTIRELY from the same publisher     : 60 (1.31%)
   RS is leave-one-POST-out over the content item, not leave-one-PUBLISHER-out.
   The 'different audience' reading therefore holds for 84.3% of the sample, not all of it. For the rest
   part of RS is the same page's own audience reacting to the same link, so
   the reviewer's definitional-artefact concern is only partly answered there.

   ICC of f_neg across content items (one-way ANOVA) = 0.701
   -> 70.1% of the variance in a post's negativity is attributable to
      which news item is being shared, which is what RS picks up.

   ICC of RS across content items (one-way ANOVA) = 0.974
   -> RS is very nearly one constant per link, which is what the content
      random intercept already absorbs.

-- the association survives inside the mixed-reaction subsample --
   mixed posts n = 3631
   Pearson (CI, RS) among mixed posts  = 0.7326
   Spearman(CI, RS) among mixed posts  = 0.7457
   Among all-positive posts CI is constant at 1.00, so they carry no
   information about the CI-RS slope; the association is estimated entirely
   on the mixed posts.
